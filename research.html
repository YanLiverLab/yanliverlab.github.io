OUR RESEARCH

Understanding the mechanisms of liver injury.

Our laboratory investigates molecular and cellular mechanisms that drive liver disease and hepatotoxicity.

01

Cholestatic Liver Injury

Cholestasis is an important component of several liver diseases. We investigate how disruption of bile acid homeostasis contributes to hepatic injury and how cholestatic pathways interact with other mechanisms of liver disease.

We are particularly interested in the relationship between bile acid accumulation, cellular stress, hepatocyte injury, and disease progression.

02

Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease

Our laboratory investigates mechanisms underlying alcohol-associated liver disease, with particular emphasis on alcohol-associated hepatitis.

A major focus is understanding how intrahepatic cholestasis and bile acid-mediated cell death contribute to liver injury and disease progression.

03

Hepatotoxicity

Environmental and chemical exposures can produce significant liver injury. Our research investigates how toxic exposures disrupt hepatic cellular pathways.

Previous work has examined perfluoroalkyl substances, including PFOA, and pathways involving lipid metabolism, insulin signaling, endoplasmic reticulum stress, autophagy, and microRNA alterations.

04

Autophagy in Liver Pathophysiology

Autophagy is an essential cellular process involved in maintaining hepatic homeostasis and metabolic function.

We investigate how autophagy influences alcohol-associated liver disease, metabolic liver disease, cholestatic injury, and hepatotoxicity.

05

Protein Condensates and Liver Disease

Our laboratory investigates the formation and biological functions of protein condensates in liver disease.

One area of interest is Mallory-Denk bodies, protein-containing structures associated with several chronic liver diseases.

06

Hepatic Microenvironment

Liver disease does not occur within individual cells in isolation. Interactions among hepatocytes, immune cells, stromal cells, and other cellular populations influence disease progression.

We investigate how changes in the hepatic microenvironment contribute to liver injury and disease.
